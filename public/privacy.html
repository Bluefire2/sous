Privacy policy

Last updated: 8 October 2026

What is stored

When you sign in, Sous associates your Google account id (sub), email address, and display name with your library. A signed session cookie (sous_session) holds your sub and email; it is HttpOnly and is not readable by page scripts. A cached copy of your profile may also be kept in this browser's local storage under cook.session, so the app can show who is signed in. The UI language preference is kept in this browser's local storage under cook.locale.

Your recipes, chat messages, cooking progress, cook log entries (the date you cooked, rating, servings, notes, lessons and photos), and attached photos are stored in Google Cloud (Firestore and Cloud Storage) in the europe-west1 region. They are loaded into this browser while you are signed in. There is no on-device recipe database. Cached translations of your recipes are stored in Firestore under your account and are deleted when their recipe is deleted. Translations of recipes shared with you are not stored; they are translated again each session. Photos used only to import a recipe are not kept.

Each account has a daily limit on Sous's AI features (Ask, the assistant, recipe import, translation, and dictation), so that a misused account cannot run up costs. To enforce it, Sous stores one record per account per day (UTC) in Firestore under your account: an estimate of what that day's AI requests cost, and how many calls to the AI model they made. It never holds what you asked or what came back.

If an import goes wrong, or you mark an import with a thumbs down, you can choose to send an import report. Nothing is sent unless you press Send report. A report is stored in Firestore (europe-west1), separately from your library, and only the owner reads it, to fix recipe import. It holds your account id (sub); how the recipe arrived; the full link you imported, including anything after a ? or #; for pasted text, the text you pasted; for a recipe written from an idea, the idea you typed; for photos, how many there were; the error you saw, if any; what the import returned, including the recipe text it extracted and codes for any problems the import check found; whether translation worked; the note you add, if any; and the app language. It never holds your email address, the photos, or the notes you typed with them. A thumbs up is not stored as a report; it is only counted in the server log described below.

If you send a suggestion from Suggest a feature, it is stored in Firestore (europe-west1), separately from your library, and only the owner reads it. It holds your account id (sub); the text you wrote; whether you allowed the owner to email you about it; the app language; whether you opened the page from your library or from Settings; and whether you use Sous as an installed app. It never holds your email address. If you allow it, the owner may reply to the email address you sign in with.

No password and no Google refresh token is stored — the app only ever asks Google who you are.

If you complete Google sign-in but are not a member, and you press Request access, Sous stores your email address, Google display name, Google account id (sub) and the request timestamps in Firestore (europe-west1) so the owner can decide. No recipe library and no users/{sub} document is created for a person who is not admitted.

If someone who already has access sends you a single-use invite link and you open it, Sous stores a hashed copy of that link (not the secret itself) until it is used, expires after 7 days, or is revoked. Redeeming it stores the same kind of membership and access-request records as an approved request. Opening an invite link does not grant access by itself — you still complete Google sign-in, and only a verified email can join. If you create an invite link, Sous stores your account id and email with it, and the owner can see who created each unused link.

Server logs

Google Cloud keeps a log of the requests made to Sous: the time, the address requested, the response, your IP address, and your browser's user agent. Since 1 October 2026, requests that open an invite link or a collection link are left out of that log, so the secret part of such a link is not recorded there. Entries written before then can still contain such a link until they are deleted automatically (see Retention and deletion). Requests that open a public collection link or a recipe link, and the requests that load its recipes and photos, are left out the same way.

Sous also writes its own log lines for errors and for a few events. Some of them include your account id (sub). Sous does not write your email address or recipe text to its logs on purpose, but an error reported by a service Sous uses can occasionally quote part of the request that failed.

Each recipe import writes one line, so failed imports can be found and fixed. The line holds your account id; whether the recipe came from a link, pasted text, photos, or the Chrome extension; for a link or the extension, the page address without anything after a ? or #; for photos, how many there were and their total size; for a link, the extension, or pasted text, whether the recipe was read from the page's structured recipe data or from its text, and how each attempt to read it went; whether the import worked and, if not, why; codes for any problems the import check found, such as missing steps; for a recipe written from an idea, whether Search the web was on and how many searches ran; how many ingredients and steps came out; whether a requested translation worked; and how long it took. It never includes your email address, the recipe text, the page itself, pasted text, the idea you typed, the searches, notes, or photos.

Rating an import or sending an import report writes one more line: your account id, whether it was a report or a thumbs up, how the recipe arrived, the site's host name (not the full address), codes for any problems the import check found, whether you added a note, the response, an error code if the report could not be saved, and how long it took. It never includes the rest of the link, the recipe text, pasted text, or your note.

Sending a suggestion writes one line: your account id, whether you opened the page from your library or from Settings, how long the text is, whether you allowed an email reply, the response, an error code if the suggestion could not be saved, and how long it took. It never includes the text.

Saving a copy from a recipe link writes one line: your account id, whether the copy was new, already in your library, or your own recipe, the response, how many photos were copied, and how long it took. It never includes the link, the recipe, or anyone's name.

Each request from a connected AI app (see Connected apps) writes one line: your account id, the app's host name, which MCP method or tool it called, the outcome, the response, how many recipes it found or returned, and how long it took. Each step of connecting an app (the authorization request, the consent page, and each token request) writes one line: the step, the app's host name, the outcome, the kind of token request, the response, and how long it took. These lines never include what the app asked for, recipe text, tokens or codes, your email address, or the full address the app returns you to.

Each request to an AI feature writes one line per call to Google's Gemini API: your account id, which feature it was, the model, how many tokens went in and came out, how many web searches ran, and the estimated cost. A request refused by the daily limit writes one line with your account id, the feature, and the reason. These lines never include what you asked, the reply, or recipe text.

Logs are used only to keep Sous running, to fix problems, and to measure how well recipe import works. They are stored in Google Cloud Logging, separately from your library and not only in the region where your library is stored.

What it is used for

Showing your own recipe library across devices, and answering cooking questions. Your information is not sold and is not used for advertising. Import reports you choose to send are used only to fix recipe import. Suggestions you send are used only to decide what to build, and, if you allow it, to reply to you.

If you share a named collection, the live recipes in that folder and their recipe photos become visible to the Google accounts whose emails you add, and they see your email address as the person who shared it. Those people must already have a Sous account. You can also create a link to the collection: anyone who already has a Sous account and opens it can join until you revoke the link or it expires after 7 days, and you then see their email address. Only a fingerprint of the link is stored; opening it does not create a Sous account. If you make someone an editor, their changes to a recipe's details (such as its title, ingredients, steps, notes, servings, cooking times, and tags) are saved to your copy of that recipe; they cannot change its photos, delete it, or change who has access. Chat messages and cooking checkboxes on a shared recipe stay in the viewer's own account. The implicit default list (recipes that are not in a named collection) is never shareable.

You can also make a named collection public. Anyone who has its public link can then read the live recipes in it and their recipe photos, without signing in and without a Sous account. The link alone does not show them your email address or account id. The link is not listed anywhere, pages opened with it ask search engines not to index them, and it keeps working until you turn it off or delete the collection; turning it on again creates a new link. Sous stores the public link with a record of which collection it opens, so you can copy it again later. Someone who opens a public link without signing in cannot use the cooking assistant, translation, or any other feature that sends text to Gemini, and cannot change anything. A signed-in member who opens it can add the collection to their library as a viewer. As with any other share, you then see their email address, and they see yours as the person who shared it.

You can also turn on a link for one of your own recipes. Anyone who has the recipe link can then read that live recipe and its photos, without signing in and without a Sous account, and the page shows your Google display name as the person who shared it (never your email address or account id). Like a public collection link, it is not listed anywhere, asks search engines not to index it, keeps working until you turn it off or delete the recipe, and is stored so you can copy it again; turning it on again creates a new link. Visitors cannot use the cooking assistant or translation from it, and cannot change your recipe. A signed-in member who opens it can save a copy to their own library: Sous copies the recipe's text and photos into their account, with your display name and the date as where it came from. That copy is theirs. It does not change when you edit or delete your recipe, turning the link off does not remove it, and deleting your account does not remove it either. You do not see who saved a copy.

Connected apps

A member can connect an AI app, such as Claude, to Sous. Connecting happens on a Sous consent page that names the app by its web address and lists what it will be able to do; nothing is connected until you press Allow. A connected app can search and read the recipes in your own library and list your collections and, if you allowed editing, add new recipes, edit your existing ones, and move them between your collections. Moving a recipe into a collection you share with other members shows it to them; a connected app never adds recipes to a collection that has a public link. It cannot see recipes shared with you, your photos, or your cook log, and it cannot delete anything.

The app's provider receives the recipe text the app asks for and the text it sends to Sous, under that provider's own terms and privacy policy. Sous does not control what the provider does with it.

For each connected app, Sous stores a grant under your account: the app's address (its client ID URL), the name it gives itself, what you allowed it to do, your email address (to check that you still have access), when you connected it, and when it was last used. The app holds access and refresh tokens; Sous stores only fingerprints (hashes) of them and of the one-time sign-in codes, never the tokens themselves. Tokens expire within 30 days unless the app renews them.

To disconnect an app, use Settings → Connected apps; it stops working on its next request. Signing out of Sous does not disconnect your apps. If you lose access to Sous, your connected apps stop working too.

What it can reach in your Google account

Sign-in uses the scopes openid, userinfo.email and userinfo.profile — your name, email address and account id, and nothing else. Sous cannot read your Gmail, Drive, Calendar, contacts or photos.

Third parties

On a recipe, the text of that recipe, your chat messages on it, and any photo you send the assistant are passed to Google's Gemini API at request time to produce a reply. When you use the library assistant (Ask, at /assistant), Sous sends many recipes from your library — titles, tags, and the full text of recipes the assistant looks up — not only the recipe you are viewing, to Google's Gemini API at request time to produce the reply. Using Dictate uploads a short microphone recording to Sous, which sends it to Gemini to turn into text and does not store the audio. Recipe text is sent to Gemini, or the configured translation provider, for translation, including at import. Photos you add on the Import screen are uploaded to Sous, which sends them to Google's Gemini API to extract the recipe. They are not stored and are not attached to the saved recipe. When you ask Sous to write a recipe from an idea, the text you type is sent to Google's Gemini API. With Search the web on, Gemini may run Google searches based on it; the pages it used are shown to you with the result and are not stored. Gemini processes these requests on Google's infrastructure, which is not limited to the europe-west1 region. Recipe import fetches the URL you paste. If you press Request access, the notification email to the owner is sent through Resend and contains your email address, display name and the time of the request; it includes no one-click approval link — approval only ever happens on the owner's own signed-in screen. If the owner approves that request, Sous sends an email through Resend to the address on the request. The email says the request was approved and links to the app so you can sign in. It includes no secret and no one-click approval. Hosting is on Google Cloud.

Retention and deletion

Deleting a recipe in Sous deletes its chat thread, cooking progress, cook log entries (dates, ratings, notes, lessons and their photos), attached photos, and cached translations from your stored library. Deleting a single cook log entry deletes its photos too.

Access records are kept as follows. A pending access request is kept until the owner decides it. A declined request is kept indefinitely, until it is deleted by hand, because that record is what stops a repeat request from notifying the owner again. An approved request is likewise kept indefinitely — it is what the owner's admin screen lists. A membership record lasts as long as the person has access; revoking access sets its status to revoked rather than deleting it, so a revoked person still has a row. Unused invite-link records are kept until they are redeemed, expire, or they are revoked; a redeemed invite keeps a hashed identifier and the Google account id that used it. A public collection link record is kept, marked as off, after you turn the link off or delete its collection, and a recipe link record after you turn it off or delete its recipe. There is no automated purge job: none of these records is deleted on a schedule.

Server logs, import reports, suggestions, daily AI usage records, and connected-app codes and tokens are the exceptions. Google Cloud Logging deletes logs automatically 30 days after they are written. Logs are not removed earlier, including by an account deletion request. Import reports are deleted automatically about 180 days after you send them, and suggestions about one year after you send them. Daily AI usage records are deleted automatically about 8 days after their day. Fingerprints of connected-app codes and tokens are deleted automatically after they expire. A connected app's grant is kept until you disconnect it; disconnecting marks it revoked.

To delete all data stored for your account, email chernyshov.k@gmail.com from the same address you use to sign in. There is no in-app delete-account button. A deletion request covers all of it: your access-request record, your membership record, any invite-link redemption recorded for your account, any import reports and suggestions you sent, any public collection links and recipe links you created, your connected apps and their tokens, and your stored library — recipes, chat messages, cooking progress, cook log entries (dates, ratings, notes, lessons and photos), photos, cached translations, and daily AI usage records — and deleting these also removes your access. It also covers collection links you made, and your email where other members' records hold it: on collections you shared and collections shared with you. Revoking Sous's access from your Google account permissions page signs you out but does not by itself delete your stored recipes.

One thing stays: your account id (sub), a number Google assigns that holds no name or email, where it is part of another member's record. That is who approved or invited a member, who decided an access request, the deleted share records on both sides of a collection you shared or that was shared with you, and the link on another member's own chat messages and cooking progress to a shared recipe of yours. Removing it would change or break their records. Nothing else about you stays, and it no longer leads to an account.

Sign out to drop the session cookie when the network request succeeds. If you sign out while offline, the cached profile is cleared from this browser, but the HttpOnly cookie may remain until it expires or a later sign-out succeeds. Your library in Google Cloud remains until you request account deletion.

Access

Sous is invitation-only: signing in is limited to allow-listed email addresses, to accounts the owner has approved from an access request, and to accounts that redeem a single-use invite link sent by someone who already has access. Requesting access does not grant it. Opening an invite link does not grant it until Google sign-in succeeds with a verified email.
